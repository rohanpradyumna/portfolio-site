The world is messy. There's too much to fix. And I can't stop building things.

It's a problem, honestly.

## The Itch

I remember the first time I shipped something that people actually used. It wasn't good — it was barely functional — but someone I'd never met was using it. They found value in something I made.

That feeling? Addictive.

## The Philosophy

Here's what I believe about building:

- **Ship the ugly version.** Seriously. Do it. Stop overthinking.
- **Your users' problems > your clever solution.** Always.
- **Small steps compound into big things.** Gym, startups, life — it's all the same.
- **Nobody cares how you built it, only that it works.**

## The Tension

There's a constant tension between wanting to build *the right thing* and wanting to *build things right*. I've learned to lean toward the former. Perfection is the enemy of progress, and progress is what matters.

> "We suffer more often in imagination than in reality." — Seneca

This applies to shipping too. The fear of putting something out there is almost always worse than the reality of doing it.

## What's Next

Right now I'm building AI tools that turn requirements into working code. It sounds ambitious because it is. But every big thing started as a seemingly impossible idea that someone decided to just... start.

So I keep building. The world stays messy, but maybe a little less so with each thing I ship.
